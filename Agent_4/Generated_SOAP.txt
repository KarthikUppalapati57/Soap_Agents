S – Subjective

Patient reports moderate to severe pain in the left knee for the past 3 weeks.
Pain worsens with physical activity: walking, climbing stairs, standing for long periods.
Occasional swelling and morning stiffness.
No fever, weight loss, chest pain, or palpitations.
Pain disrupts sleep.
Past medical history: well-controlled hypertension.
Medications: antihypertensive only.

O – Objective

Vitals: BP 128/82 mmHg, HR 72 bpm, RR 16, Temp 98.6°F.
Left knee: mild swelling, tenderness around joint sides, no visible deformity.
Range of motion: reduced flexion and extension due to pain.
Stability tests: Lachman’s negative, McMurray’s negative. Slight discomfort with varus/valgus stress tests.

A – Assessment

Probable exacerbation of mild osteoarthritis in the left knee.
Differential includes other causes of knee pain (e.g., meniscal injury) but stability tests negative.

P – Plan

Order X-ray of left knee; consider MRI if inconclusive.
Start physical therapy for strengthening and flexibility.
Lifestyle modifications: avoid activities that worsen pain, consider cane or knee brace.
Pain management: OTC NSAIDs as needed.
Follow-up: in 4 weeks to evaluate response to treatment.
